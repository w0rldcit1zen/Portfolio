01

Audience Growth at Global Scale

Strategy · Multi-channel delivery · Measurable impact

Audience growth at this scale is never an accident. It is the result of brutal discipline and clear strategy. At WMO, total following sat at roughly 200,000; four years later it broke 1.3 million. We shut off the institutional noise — repositioned the channel strategy, prioritized LinkedIn to reach policymakers directly, and built a content machine a small team could actually run.

The deeper win was a deliberate act of framing. #StateOfClimate already existed as a conversation, but it skewed negative and was wrapped in jargon that repelled the people it needed. Communication research is clear: doom-and-gloom loses to framing built on benefit, security, and agency. We made the science legible — partnering with climate scientist Ed Hawkins to put his warming stripes at the heart of the visual narrative — and mentions rose 661%, at 4.6% engagement against a 2–3% sector benchmark.

Result

200K → 1.3M followers

Audience grew 6.5x in four years through structural channel repositioning and a storytelling-first approach.

02

From Broadcast to Predictive Community

Community strategy · Social intelligence · ROI discipline

The era of the one-way broadcast is over. We led WMO's transition from a Broadcast Model to a Community Model, and the data proved it: a 600% increase in inbound messages, with engagement growth (+117.7%) dramatically outpacing impression growth (+94.1%). The groundwork we architected points to the next frontier — social listening as a predictive early-warning system for emerging narratives and reputational risk.

A conviction sits under this work. Everyone wants to go viral; almost nobody connects. In the anxious, always-on limbo of scrolling feeds and video calls, the audience is a moving target — and you hit it with consistency, not stunts. Adapt, adjust, listen, move; but stay fluid, disciplined, calm, and persistent. The authentic outlasts the trending, every time.

We also put a number on the function itself, valuing the channel portfolio at approximately $6.4 million USD using standard CPA, CPC, and CPM benchmarks — the market replacement cost of the audience and reach. Communication defended in the language executives actually use: assets and return.

03

Changing the Conversation

Narrative strategy · Human-centered storytelling · Multi-agency orchestration

When we took on Early Warnings for All — the UN mandate to protect every person on Earth with early warning systems by 2027 — the conversation belonged to technical experts. Missing were the people who need those systems most: countries facing extreme weather of rising frequency and intensity. We introduced storytelling, and for the first time they showed the world what early warnings actually look like — not just phone alerts, but flags on beaches for fishers, radio bulletins, church bells. Within a year, mentions grew more than tenfold, from 7.5 million to 80 million.

It required a hard narrative choice. In a moment when national interest has overtaken international cooperation — hopefully only for now — early warnings had to speak the language of security: food security, lives and livelihoods against flash floods, cyclones, drought, and heatwaves. And resilience, because adaptation moves policy where mitigation stalls — and it is the entry point to mitigation anyway: better early warnings mean better climate intelligence, and collective understanding rises. We led communications across the multi-agency task force with IFRC, UNDRR, and ITU, aligning massive agencies behind a single voice — then did the unglamorous thing that wins: we repeated it, everywhere, until it stuck. Framing opens the door; discipline walks through it.

04

Award-Winning AI Product

AI product launch · Frontier-tech storytelling · Information integrity

The knowledge was never missing — it was buried. Thousands of PDFs, scattered webpages, files named final_final_v2: the world's authoritative climate and weather intelligence, hidden precisely when the highest-stakes policy decisions needed it instantly. AskWMO changed that — ask a question, get an authoritative answer drawn straight from WMO's foundational document base, no digging through folders. We were first, and it showed where institutional knowledge is heading.

And access is the integrity battle: when the right science is hard to reach, misinformation — the honest kind of wrong — festers, and disinformation — the deliberate kind — triumphs. For this work, the founder was one of four named recipients of the 2024 Royal Meteorological Society Award for Impact on Science, Policy or Society — leading the digital communication strategy and launch narrative for a frontier-tech tool that reinforced scientific authority rather than degrading it.

AI-Native Practice: Answer Engines & Agentic Systems

GEO / AEO · Applied AI · The AI content supply chain

The audience is no longer only human. Increasingly, answers come from AI chatbots — and an institution absent from the sources those models draw on is invisible at the moment of decision. Primo has built a complete GEO/AEO framework for this reality: content engineered for machine extraction, mapped to the off-site sources that actually feed the models, across Western and Chinese AI ecosystems alike. AskWMO was the early instinct; the framework is the developed method, ready to deploy. The deeper point: information behavior keeps migrating — from the library card catalog to the search engine to the chatbot — and communication has to move with it. Meeting people where they ask is the discipline; only the destination changed.

And we build, not just advise. The founder architected an autonomous AI agent producing a daily strategic intelligence brief — defined voice, scheduled pipeline, strict budget — and is developing an AI content supply chain: one source of truth fanned into platform-native, GEO-optimized assets, with a human always in the loop for judgment.
