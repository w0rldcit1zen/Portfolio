01

Audience Growth at Global Scale

Strategy · Integrated multi-channel delivery · Measurable impact

Audience growth at this scale is never an accident. It is the result of brutal discipline and clear strategy. At WMO, the total following across all channels sat at roughly 200,000. Four years later, it broke 1.3 million. We achieved that by shutting off the institutional noise — repositioning the channel strategy, prioritizing LinkedIn to reach policymakers directly, and building a content machine a small team could actually run. We shifted the focus from the organization to the people affected by climate on the ground: donors, youth, and local beneficiaries.

The numbers make the case, but the figure alone is not the achievement. #StateOfClimate was a conversation that already existed — but it skewed negative, wrapped in science so dull and jargon-heavy it repelled the very people it needed to reach. The 661% rise in mentions was not about volume; it was about changing the character of the conversation, shifting sentiment and bringing in voices that had never engaged.

The fix was a deliberate act of framing. Decades of communication research confirm that facts alone do not move people, and that doom-and-gloom "sacrifice" messaging loses to framing built on benefit, security, and agency. We made the science legible — partnering with climate scientist Ed Hawkins to put his now-iconic warming stripes at the heart of the visual narrative, a graphic proven to spark conversations in the very places they normally never happen. A 4.6% engagement rate against a 2–3% sector benchmark is what happens when dense science is finally made to feel human.

200K → 1.3M followers

Total audience across all channels grew 6.5x in four years through structural channel repositioning and a storytelling-first approach.

From Broadcast to Predictive Community

Community strategy · Social intelligence · Predictive posture

The era of the one-way broadcast is over. The organizations that win now are the ones that listen, learn, and respond in real time — treating social not as a megaphone but as a living, two-way intelligence system. We led exactly that transition at WMO, and the data proved it worked.

We moved from a Broadcast Model to a Community Model, and the signal was unmistakable: a 600% increase in inbound messages, with engagement growth (+117.7%) dramatically outpacing impression growth (+94.1%). Content was no longer just being served by algorithms; it was stopping the scroll and triggering dialogue. The next frontier, whose groundwork we architected, is predictive — turning social listening into an early-warning system for emerging narratives, competitive moves, and reputational risk, surfaced in minutes, not weeks.

03

Creating a New Conversation

Narrative strategy · Category creation · Multi-agency orchestration

When we took on Early Warnings for All — the UN mandate to protect every person on Earth with early warning systems by 2027 — online conversation about the topic was effectively zero. Run a boolean search before we began, and you found near silence. We didn't ride a trend; we built a category. Within a year, mentions had grown more than tenfold, from 7.5 million to 80 million. That figure is not a growth rate — it is the measure of a conversation that did not exist before we built it.

Building it required a hard narrative choice. Doom-and-gloom framing does not move governments, especially when multilateralism is cracking under pressure. We anchored the initiative to something undeniable: resilience and security — reframing early warnings from a humanitarian sunk cost into a sovereign investment, reaching inward-looking governments through the language of national security and adaptation they already cared about. The research backs this precisely: a security frame consistently outperforms a distant climate frame in driving real support. We led WMO's communications across the multi-agency task force with IFRC, UNDRR, and ITU, building the messaging framework, visual identity, and media assets that aligned massive agencies behind a single voice.

Communication as an Owned Asset

ROI discipline · Economic valuation · Executive reporting

Communication is too often treated as a cost center — a soft function that spends money and reports vanity metrics. We reject that framing. A social media portfolio is an owned digital asset with a real, defensible market value, and treating it that way changes every conversation with leadership.

We built the economic case at WMO using standard digital advertising benchmarks — Cost Per Acquisition, Cost Per Click, Cost Per Mille — to value the channel portfolio at approximately $6.4 million USD: the market replacement cost to rebuild that audience, traffic, and reach through paid media. This is the discipline a modern communication function demands — a clear return for every dollar spent, defended in the language executives actually use.

Engineering for the Answer Engine

GEO / AEO · LLM-source strategy · The next search frontier

The audience is no longer only human. Increasingly, people get their answers from AI chatbots — and if an institution is not present in the sources those models draw from, it is invisible at the exact moment of decision. Generative and Answer Engine Optimization (GEO/AEO) are the disciplines of becoming a cited source inside ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Grok, and their Chinese counterparts. This is where search is going, and most communication functions have not started.

Primo has built a full GEO/AEO framework for this new reality — content engineered for machine extraction (definition-led architecture, self-contained answer blocks, structured data, authoritative citation) mapped to the off-site source strategy that actually feeds these models: Reddit and Quora for sentiment, LinkedIn for professional recognition via Copilot, X for real-time signal, and the distinct Chinese ecosystem (Zhihu, Baidu Baike, WeChat) that trains Ernie, Kimi, and Qwen. The instinct is not new — AskWMO was, in effect, an early answer-engine play. The framework is developed and ready to deploy at scale.
